The short answer
  1. Get out fast when the smoke alarm sounds. Don't fight the fire or grab belongings.
  2. Stay low under smoke, and feel doors with the back of your hand before opening them.
  3. Close doors behind you, go to your outside meeting place, and call 911 from outside.
  4. Once you're out, stay out. Never go back in.
  5. If you're trapped, close the door, seal the gaps, call 911 and signal from a window.

How to escape a house fire

  1. Get out first. Fire spreads fast, and you may have as little as two minutes. Leave everything behind.
  2. Test doors. Feel the door with the back of your hand. If it's hot or smoke is coming under it, use another way out. If it's cool, open it slowly.
  3. Stay low. Crawl under smoke, where the air is cleaner and cooler. Cover your nose and mouth with a cloth if you can.
  4. Close doors behind you to slow the fire and smoke.
  5. Use stairs, never elevators, in an apartment building.
  6. Go to your meeting place outside and call 911.
  7. Stay out. Tell firefighters if anyone is still inside; don't go back in yourself.

If your clothes catch fire: stop, drop and roll. Stop where you are, drop to the ground, cover your face with your hands, and roll until the flames are out. Running feeds the fire.

If you're trapped

  • Close the door between you and the fire
  • Seal gaps around doors and vents with towels, clothes or duct tape
  • Call 911 and say exactly where you are
  • Stay near a window and close to the floor
  • Signal firefighters with a light-colored cloth or a flashlight at the window

How to make a home fire escape plan

  • Draw a map of your home with two ways out of every room, usually a door and a window
  • Make sure windows open easily and that screens and security bars can be removed from inside
  • Pick an outside meeting place a safe distance away, such as a tree, light pole or mailbox
  • Put smoke alarms on every level, outside sleeping areas and in every bedroom
  • Make sure everyone knows the sound of the smoke alarm
  • Sleep with bedroom doors closed to slow smoke and fire
  • Assign someone to help babies, older adults and anyone with a disability
  • Practice the plan at least twice a year, including at night

After a house fire

  • Don't go back in until the fire department says it's safe.
  • Get medical care for burns or if anyone breathed in smoke and has trouble breathing, coughing or confusion.
  • Call your insurance company, and the Red Cross if you need somewhere to stay.
  • Throw out food, drinks and medicines exposed to heat, smoke or soot.

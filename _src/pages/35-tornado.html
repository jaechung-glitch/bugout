The short answer

When a tornado warning is issued, go right away to a basement, storm shelter or safe room. If you don't have one, go to a small interior room on the lowest floor, such as a closet, bathroom or hallway, away from windows. Cover your head and neck with your arms, a mattress or heavy blankets. Leave a mobile home for a sturdy building. In a car, drive to shelter if you can; if not, crouch down and cover your head, or lie in a ditch. Never shelter under an overpass.

Tornado watch vs tornado warning

AlertWhat it meansWhat to do
Tornado watchTornadoes are possible in and near the area, often several counties or states.Review your plan, check your shelter and supplies, keep alerts on.
Tornado warningA tornado has been sighted or indicated by radar, for an area about the size of a city or small county.Take shelter now.

Where to shelter, wherever you are

Where you areWhat to do
HouseBasement, safe room or a small interior room on the lowest floor, away from windows. Bring pets if there's time.
Apartment or high-riseGo to the lowest floor you can reach, to an interior hallway or room without windows. Use the stairs, not the elevator.
Work or schoolFollow your tornado drill to the shelter area. Avoid large open rooms such as cafeterias, gyms and auditoriums.
Mobile home or tentLeave now for a sturdy building or storm shelter. Neither is safe in a tornado.
CarDrive to the nearest sturdy shelter. If you can't, crouch down in the car and cover your head, or get out and lie in a ditch or low spot, covering your head.
OutdoorsGet inside a sturdy building now. Sheds and storage buildings aren't safe.

How to protect yourself in your shelter

  • Get as low as possible and put as many walls as you can between you and the outside.
  • Cover your head and neck with your arms. A mattress, heavy blankets or a helmet add protection from flying debris.
  • Wear sturdy shoes if you can grab them; you may have to walk over debris afterward.
  • Stay in your shelter until the warning expires or officials say it's safe.

After a tornado

  • Watch for downed power lines and stay away from them.
  • Stay out of damaged buildings; they can collapse. If you smell gas, get out and call 911 from outside.
  • Use flashlights, not candles.
  • Wear sturdy shoes, long sleeves and gloves when moving through debris.
  • Text family that you're safe, and check on neighbors.
  • Never run a generator indoors.

How to get ready before tornado season

  • Pick your shelter spot at home and practice getting there in under a minute.
  • Turn on emergency alerts on your phone, and own a NOAA Weather Radio for warnings at night.
  • Keep shoes, a flashlight, a whistle and helmets near your shelter spot.
  • Keep an emergency kit stocked for several days without power.

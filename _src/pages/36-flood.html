The short answer
  1. If you're in a flood-prone area and a flood or flash flood warning is issued, move to higher ground right away.
  2. Leave immediately if officials tell you to evacuate.
  3. Never drive or walk into floodwater. Six inches of moving water can knock you down, and 12 inches can carry away most cars.
  4. If you're trapped by rising water, go to the highest point you can and call 911.
  5. Stay away from water near outlets or power lines, and wait for officials before returning home.

Turn around, don't drown

The National Weather Service says more deaths occur from flooding each year than from any other thunderstorm hazard, and the CDC reports that over half of flood drownings happen when someone drives into floodwater.

  • Never drive around barriers. The road under the water may be gone.
  • Be extra careful at night, when it's hard to see how deep water is.
  • Keep children away from floodwater, storm drains and creeks.

What to do during a flood

  • Keep a battery or hand-crank radio or NOAA Weather Radio on for updates.
  • If you're camping or living in a low-lying area, get to higher ground immediately.
  • If told to evacuate, lock your home and, if there's time, turn off utilities and unplug appliances. See what to do when told to evacuate.
  • If your car stalls in rising water, get out and move to higher ground if you can do so safely.
  • If trapped in a building, go to the highest level you can and call 911.
  • Don't go into a basement or any room where water covers outlets or cords. If you see sparks or hear buzzing, crackling or popping, get out.

After a flood

  • Go home only when officials say it's safe.
  • Stay out of floodwater: it can hide debris and carry sewage and chemicals, and it may be electrified by downed lines.
  • Stay out of buildings that are still surrounded by water, and leave if you smell gas.
  • Follow any boil-water notice. Our water guide shows how to make water safe.
  • Throw out food, including canned food with damaged cans, that touched floodwater.
  • Never run a generator indoors while drying out your home.

How to prepare for a flood

  • Find out whether your home is in a flood zone and plan a route to higher ground.
  • Standard homeowners insurance usually doesn't cover floods; ask about flood insurance.
  • Keep important documents in a waterproof bag, and a go bag ready.
  • Turn on emergency alerts on your phone.

The short answer
  1. Stop. Don't keep walking. Sit, calm down, and think.
  2. Try to retrace your steps only if you're sure of the way back. Otherwise, stay put.
  3. Call 911 if you have any signal, and give landmarks and your GPS coordinates.
  4. Signal: three blasts on a whistle, bright clothing in the open, a fire if it's allowed and safe.
  5. Get out of the wind, wet and cold before dark.

What to do when you're lost

  1. Stop, think, observe, plan. Panic makes people walk faster in the wrong direction. Drink some water, eat something, and take stock.
  2. Try to orient yourself. Check your map, compass or GPS app. Look for trail markers, and backtrack carefully only if you're confident.
  3. Stay put if you're not sure. Moving makes you harder to find. Leave only to find water, and return to the same spot.
  4. Call for help. Call 911 if you have any signal. Give the trail name, landmarks and your coordinates. If a call won't connect, try texting 911. Then save your battery.
  5. Make yourself visible. Move to the nearest open area. Spread bright clothing or gear where it can be seen from above.
  6. Signal in threes. Three whistle blasts, three shouts or three flashes is the universal distress call. Repeat at intervals.
  7. Prepare for the night. Shelter comes before food: get out of wind and off cold ground well before dark.

How to get through a night outside

  • Put on every layer you have, and stay dry. Wet clothing pulls heat away fast.
  • Insulate yourself from the ground with branches, leaves or your pack. The ground draws heat out of your body.
  • Shelter against a rock, log or dense trees, out of the wind.
  • Build a small fire only if fires are allowed and it's safe; it gives warmth and helps searchers spot you.
  • Ration nothing important: drink water when you need it. You can last weeks without food but only days without water.

If a child gets lost

Teach children to hug a tree: stay in one place, keep warm and dry, and make themselves big and visible when they hear people. Give every child a whistle and tell them to blow it three times if they're lost. If a member of your group goes missing, stop, go back to the last place you saw them, and call 911.

How not to get lost

  • Leave a trip plan with someone: where you're going, your route and when you'll be back
  • Carry a paper map and compass, and know how to use them
  • Download offline maps before you lose signal
  • Carry a whistle, a mirror and a headlamp
  • Pack extra water, food and a warm layer, even on short hikes
  • Bring a charged phone and a power bank
  • Wear bright clothing
  • Agree on a meet-up spot if your group gets separated
  • Look back often so you'll recognize the trail on the way out

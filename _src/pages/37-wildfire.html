The short answer
  1. Leave early: as soon as officials suggest it, or whenever you feel in danger. Don't wait for a mandatory order.
  2. If you have a few minutes, close windows and doors (unlocked), leave lights on, turn off the gas and bring in anything that burns.
  3. Trapped in a car: park away from vegetation, close windows and vents, cover yourself with a wool blanket, lie on the floor and call 911.
  4. Trapped at home: fill tubs and sinks with water, stay inside away from windows and call 911.
  5. Return only when officials say it's safe, and watch for hot ash and downed lines.

When to leave

Go as soon as fire officials suggest it. CAL FIRE says it's safer to leave before a mandatory order, and the Red Cross warns you may never get an official notice. Waiting can leave you driving through smoke on crowded roads.

  • Keep your gas tank full during fire season, and park facing out.
  • Plan at least two ways out of your neighborhood.
  • Turn on emergency alerts, and keep a battery radio and backup phone battery.

If you have time before you leave

  • Close all windows and doors, but leave them unlocked
  • Leave lights on so firefighters can see your house in smoke
  • Turn off gas at the meter and pilot lights
  • Turn off the air conditioning
  • Take down flammable curtains; close metal shutters
  • Move furniture to the middle of rooms, away from windows and doors
  • Bring in patio furniture, doormats and trash cans; shut off propane tanks
  • Connect garden hoses and fill buckets, but don't leave sprinklers running
  • Load your go bag, and keep pets close

If you're trapped by a wildfire

WhereWhat to do
In a carStay calm. Park somewhere clear of vegetation. Close all windows and vents. Cover yourself with a wool blanket or jacket, lie on the floor and call 911.
At homeStay calm and gather everyone. Call 911. Fill sinks and tubs with cold water. Keep doors and windows closed but unlocked. Stay inside, away from outside walls and windows.
On footStay calm. Get to a clear area, such as a ditch or flat ground with little to burn. Lie face down, cover your body and call 911.

Wildfire smoke

  • Smoke is a mix of pollutants that can harm anyone, and most of all children, pregnant women and people with asthma or heart or lung disease.
  • Stay indoors with windows and doors closed when smoke is heavy, and follow your local air quality alerts.
  • Wear an N95 respirator when cleaning up ash, with gloves, long sleeves, long pants and goggles.

Returning home

  • Wait until officials say it's safe.
  • Avoid hot ash, charred trees and smoldering debris. Hidden hot spots in the ground can burn you.
  • Stay away from damaged or downed power lines.
  • Check with your health department before drinking tap water.
  • Throw out food exposed to heat, smoke or fumes. When in doubt, throw it out.
  • Ask your doctor about refrigerated medicines that may have been affected.

Wildfire go checklist

  • A go bag for each person with 3 days of supplies
  • A month of medicines
  • Copies of IDs, insurance and medical records
  • Phone chargers and a backup battery
  • N95 respirators
  • A wool blanket in the car
  • Pet carriers, leashes and food
  • Two evacuation routes, and a place to stay
  • Full gas tank

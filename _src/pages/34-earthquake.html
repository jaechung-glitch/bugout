The short answer

Drop to your hands and knees, cover your head and neck under a sturdy table or desk, and hold on until the shaking stops. Don't run outside and don't stand in a doorway. In bed, stay there and cover your head with a pillow. Driving, pull over away from overpasses and power lines and stay in the car. Afterward, expect aftershocks, put on shoes, and if you're near the coast, head for high ground right away.

What to do during the shaking

  1. Drop to your hands and knees before the shaking knocks you down.
  2. Cover your head and neck under a sturdy table or desk. If there's none nearby, crawl next to an interior wall, away from windows, and cover your head and neck with your arms.
  3. Hold on to the table, or to your head and neck, until the shaking stops. Be ready to move with the table.

DrivingStop somewhere clear of buildings, trees, overpasses and wires, and stay in the car.
Wheelchair or walkerLock the wheels, stay seated, bend over and protect your head and neck.
Near the coastDrop, cover and hold on, then go to high ground or inland as soon as the shaking stops. Don't wait for a warning.

What not to do

  • Don't stand in a doorway. In modern buildings doorways are no stronger than anywhere else, and they don't shield you from falling objects.
  • Don't run outside. The ground is moving, and glass and debris fall from buildings.
  • Don't use the "triangle of life." Experts have rejected it, and the Great ShakeOut calls it potentially life-threatening.

What to do after an earthquake

  • Pause, look for hazards, then move. Put on sturdy shoes before walking on broken glass.
  • Expect aftershocks. Drop, cover and hold on each time.
  • Check yourself and others for injuries.
  • If the building is damaged, leave carefully and watch for falling hazards outside.
  • If you smell gas or hear hissing, don't use matches, lighters, appliances or light switches. Get out and call 911 from outside, then your gas company.
  • Use flashlights, not candles.
  • If you're trapped, cover your mouth and nose, and signal by blowing a whistle or tapping on a pipe or wall rather than shouting.
  • Text family that you're safe; texts get through more easily than calls.

Earthquake checklist

  • Practice drop, cover and hold on with everyone at home
  • Strap TVs, bookcases, water heaters and tall furniture to the wall
  • Sturdy shoes, a flashlight and a whistle by each bed
  • Know how to shut off your gas, water and electricity
  • Water: 1 gallon per person per day, 2 weeks if you can
  • Food that needs no cooking, and a manual can opener
  • A first-aid kit and a month of medicines
  • Dust masks and work gloves
  • A battery or hand-crank radio and power banks
  • A go bag with 3 days of supplies
  • Copies of important documents
  • Check whether your insurance covers earthquakes

How long to be ready for

56 gallons of water

  • 56 one-gallon jugs, or about 18 cases of 24 half-liter (16.9 oz) bottles
  • 168 meals of food that needs no refrigeration and little or no cooking

For 4 people for 2 weeks, at 1 gallon per person per day.

Print

How the calculator works

  • Water: 1 gallon per person per day, the CDC's minimum, for drinking and basic hygiene. A normally active person needs about three-quarters of a gallon of fluid a day; the rest is for hygiene.
  • Hot weather: Ready.gov says water needs can double in very hot temperatures, so the box doubles the total.
  • Food: 3 meals per person per day, from food that won't spoil without a fridge and needs little or no cooking.
  • Bottles: a case of 24 half-liter (16.9 oz) bottles holds about 3.2 gallons.

Add extra for children, nursing mothers, people who are sick and pets, who may need more, and for any medical needs.

How to store it

  • Buy sealed bottled water and keep it in a cool, dark placeIt lasts until the expiration date on the label.
  • Or fill your own food-grade containers from a treated tapNever reuse containers that held chemicals, such as bleach or pesticides.
  • Clean them first with dish soap, then sanitize with 1 teaspoon of unscented bleach per quart of water
  • Label each container with the date you filled it
  • Replace water you bottled yourself every 6 months

If you run short

  • Don't ration drinking water unless officials tell you to. Drink what you need today and look for more for tomorrow.
  • Cut how much your body needs by resting and staying cool.
  • Skip alcohol and caffeinated drinks: they make dehydration worse.
  • Your water heater tank and the toilet tank (not the bowl) hold usable water. Our water guide shows how to get it out and how to treat any water you're unsure of.

The food side

FEMA suggests at least several days of non-perishable food. Store what your household already eats: canned meat, fish, beans, soup, fruit and vegetables, peanut butter, crackers, cereal, nuts and shelf-stable milk, plus a manual can opener. Put new cans at the back and use the oldest first. Our prepping for beginners guide has a full list.

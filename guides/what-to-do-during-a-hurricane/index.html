What to do during a hurricane

Water, not wind, kills most people in hurricanes. This guide covers what a watch and a warning mean, when to leave, where to shelter if you stay, and how to stay safe once the storm has passed.

By the BugOut team. Updated November 12, 2026. Checked against National Weather Service, National Hurricane Center and American Red Cross guidance.

The short answer
  1. When a watch is issued, finish getting ready: supplies, gas, medicines, and your evacuation route.
  2. If officials order you to evacuate, leave right away. Storm surge and flooding cause most hurricane deaths.
  3. If you stay, shelter in a small interior room on the lowest floor, away from windows. Don't go out when the eye passes.
  4. Never walk or drive through floodwater: 12 inches of moving water can carry away most cars.
  5. After the storm, watch for downed power lines and gas leaks, and never run a generator indoors.

Why water is the biggest danger

A National Hurricane Center study of U.S. hurricane and tropical storm deaths from 1963 to 2012 found that storm surge caused 49% and rain flooding 27%. Wind caused only 8%. Storm surge is seawater pushed ashore by the storm, and it can rise faster than you can get out. If you live in a surge zone, staying put is the most dangerous choice.

Before the hurricane

  • Find out whether you live in an evacuation zone, and plan where you'd go and how.
  • Keep your gas tank at least half full during hurricane season.
  • Cover windows. Permanent storm shutters are best; otherwise 5/8-inch exterior-grade or marine plywood cut to fit, the National Weather Service says.
  • Bring in or tie down outdoor furniture, grills and anything the wind could throw.
  • Stock at least 3 days of water (1 gallon per person per day) and food, plus medicines, flashlights, batteries, a radio and a first-aid kit. Plan for two weeks without power.
  • Charge phones and power banks, and get cash.
  • Bring pets inside and pack food, carriers and records for them.

During the hurricane

  • If ordered to evacuate, leave immediately. See what to do when told to evacuate.
  • If you stay, go to a small interior room, closet or hallway on the lowest floor, with as many walls between you and the outside as possible.
  • Stay away from windows, skylights and glass doors.
  • If the eye passes over, expect a short calm, then winds from the opposite direction. Stay inside.
  • Keep a battery or hand-crank radio on for updates.
  • If water starts rising in your home, move to a higher floor and call 911 if you can.

After the hurricane

  • Return home only when officials say it's safe.
  • Stay off flooded roads and washed-out bridges. Six inches of moving water can knock you down; 12 inches can carry away most cars.
  • Stay away from downed power lines. Walk around the outside of your home to check for loose lines, gas leaks and damage before going in.
  • Stay out of a building if you smell gas or floodwater surrounds it.
  • Use flashlights, not candles. Turn the flashlight on before you go into a dark building, since a spark could ignite leaking gas.
  • Never run a generator in your home or garage. See our power outage checklist for the carbon monoxide rules.
  • Follow any boil-water notice, and throw out food that's been in floodwater or without power too long.

Hurricane checklist

  • Know your evacuation zone, route and destination
  • Gas tank at least half full
  • Water: 1 gallon per person per day, at least 3 days
  • Food that needs no cooking, and a manual can opener
  • Medicines and a first-aid kit
  • Flashlights, extra batteries and a battery or hand-crank radio
  • Charged phones and power banks
  • Cash
  • Copies of insurance papers and IDs in a waterproof bag
  • Window coverings ready
  • Outdoor furniture and grills brought in or tied down
  • Pet food, carriers and records

Quick answers

What is the difference between a hurricane watch and a hurricane warning?

A hurricane watch means hurricane conditions (sustained winds of 74 mph or more) are possible, issued 48 hours before tropical-storm-force winds are expected. A hurricane warning means they are expected, issued 36 hours ahead. A watch is the time to get ready; a warning is the time to finish and leave if told to.

Where is the safest place to be during a hurricane?

If you aren't told to evacuate, the National Weather Service says to shelter in a small interior room, closet or hallway on the lowest floor, with as many walls as possible between you and the outside, away from windows, skylights and glass doors. If you're in a storm surge or flood zone, or a mobile home, leave instead.

What kills the most people in a hurricane?

Water. A National Hurricane Center study of U.S. deaths from 1963 to 2012 found storm surge caused 49% and rain flooding 27%, while wind caused 8%. That's why evacuation orders focus on surge zones and why you should never drive into floodwater.

Should I go outside during the eye of a hurricane?

No. The calm as the eye passes is short, and the winds return quickly at hurricane force from the opposite direction. Stay sheltered until officials say the storm has passed.

When should I evacuate for a hurricane?

Leave immediately if officials order it, and leave early: roads flood and fill with traffic before the storm arrives. Keep your gas tank at least half full during hurricane season so you can go at short notice.

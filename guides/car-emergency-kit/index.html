Car emergency kit: what to keep in your car

Breakdowns, storms and long closures happen far from home. Here's what to keep in the car year-round, what to add for winter, how to check the car before you go, and what to do if you're stuck.

By the BugOut team. Updated November 5, 2026. Checked against NHTSA and Ready.gov guidance.

The short answer

Keep jumper cables, a flashlight, flares or reflective triangles, a first-aid kit, a phone charger, a blanket, water and snacks, and a paper map in the car all year. For winter, add a snow shovel, an ice scraper, sand or kitty litter, and warm clothes, and keep the gas tank close to full. If you're stranded, stay with the car.

The car emergency kit checklist

Adapted from NHTSA's and Ready.gov's lists. Pack them in a bag or bin that stays in the trunk.

Safety and repair

  • Jumper cablesCold weather drains batteries. In the cold, engines need more battery power to start.
  • Flashlight, plus extra batteries
  • Flares, reflective triangles or other emergency markers
  • A whistle, to signal for help
  • A paper map of the areaPhones die and lose signal.

First aid and comfort

  • A first-aid kit, including a tourniquetNHTSA's list includes a tourniquet. Learn to use one in a Stop the Bleed® class.
  • Any medicines you or your passengers need
  • Bottled water
  • Non-perishable snacks
  • A blanket
  • A phone and a car charger

Winter add-ons

  • A snow shovel, broom and ice scraper
  • Sand or kitty litterPour it under the tires for traction if you get stuck.
  • Extra blankets and warm clothes: hats, gloves and dry socks
  • Winter washer fluid with de-icer, with the reservoir fullOne snowstorm can use a lot of it.

Keep the gas tank close to full whenever you can, or an electric car charged. A full tank helps in an evacuation or a power outage, and it keeps the fuel line from freezing.

Check the car before winter

CheckWhat to look for
Tire pressurePressure drops as it gets colder. Inflate to the number on the label in the driver's door frame, not the maximum printed on the tire, and check when the tires haven't been driven for at least 3 hours.
TreadAt least 2/32 of an inch. Put a penny in the tread with Lincoln's head upside down: if you can see the top of his head, replace the tire. Consider winter tires, or chains or studs where they're required.
BatteryHave a mechanic check the battery, charging system and belts. Electric and hybrid cars lose range in the cold.
Lights and wipersCheck headlights, brake lights, turn signals and flashers. Test the defroster and replace worn wiper blades.
CoolantEnough coolant (antifreeze) that meets your owner's manual, with no leaks.
RecallsLook up your vehicle on NHTSA's recall lookup. Recall repairs are free.

If you're stranded

  1. Call 911 if you're in danger or someone is hurt.
  2. Stay in the car. It's your shelter. Don't wear yourself out.
  3. Make the car visible. Put something bright on the antenna or in a window, and keep the dome light on.
  4. Prevent carbon monoxide poisoning. Clear snow from the exhaust pipe, and run the engine only now and then, just long enough to stay warm. Don't run it for long with the windows up or in an enclosed space.
  5. Stay warm. Use the blankets and warm clothes in your kit.

Kids, car seats and winter coats

A bulky coat can stop a car seat harness from fitting snugly. Dress children in thin, warm layers, buckle the harness snug, then put a blanket or their coat over the top.

Other kits to keep

Ready.gov suggests supplies for home, work and the car, since you don't know where you'll be when something happens. Our bug out bag list covers a 72-hour bag for evacuations, and the winter storm checklist covers the house.

Quick answers

What should be in a car emergency kit?

Jumper cables, a flashlight, flares or reflective triangles, a first-aid kit (NHTSA suggests one that includes a tourniquet) and any medicines you need, a phone car charger, a blanket, bottled water, non-perishable snacks and a paper map. In winter, add a snow shovel, broom and ice scraper, sand or kitty litter for traction, and warm clothes.

What should I keep in my car in winter?

A snow shovel, broom and ice scraper, sand or kitty litter in case you get stuck, blankets and warm clothes, a flashlight, jumper cables, water and snacks, and a phone charger. Keep the gas tank close to full, or an electric car charged, and fill the washer fluid with winter fluid that has de-icer.

What do you do if you get stranded in your car in a snowstorm?

Stay with the car and don't overexert yourself. Make it visible with something bright on the antenna or a window and the dome light on. Clear snow from the exhaust pipe, and run the engine only now and then, just long enough to stay warm. Don't run it for long with the windows up or in an enclosed space.

How do I check if my tires are safe for winter?

Inflate them to the pressure on the label in the driver's door frame, not the number on the tire, and check when the tires are cold. Tread should be at least 2/32 of an inch: put a penny in the tread with Lincoln's head upside down, and if you can see the top of his head, it's time for new tires.

Emergency water calculator: how much water and food to store

Enter how many people live with you and how long you want to be ready for. The calculator uses the CDC's rule of 1 gallon per person per day, and turns it into jugs, bottle cases and meals.

By the BugOut team. Updated November 5, 2026. Checked against CDC and Ready.gov guidance.

How long to be ready for

56 gallons of water

  • 56 one-gallon jugs, or about 18 cases of 24 half-liter (16.9 oz) bottles
  • 168 meals of food that needs no refrigeration and little or no cooking

For 4 people for 2 weeks, at 1 gallon per person per day.

How the calculator works

  • Water: 1 gallon per person per day, the CDC's minimum, for drinking, cooking and brushing teeth. Ready.gov notes that a normally active person needs about three-quarters of a gallon of fluid a day, and that needs vary with age, health, activity, diet and climate.
  • Hot weather: Ready.gov says water needs can double in very hot temperatures, so the box doubles the total.
  • Food: 3 meals per person per day, from food that won't spoil without a fridge and needs little or no cooking.
  • Bottles: a case of 24 half-liter (16.9 oz) bottles holds about 3.2 gallons.

Add extra for children, pregnant women, nursing mothers, people who are sick and pets, who may need more, and for any medical needs.

How to store it

  • Buy sealed bottled water and keep it in a cool, dark placeIt lasts until the expiration date on the label.
  • Or fill your own food-grade containers from a treated tapNever reuse containers that held chemicals, such as bleach or pesticides.
  • Sanitize them before filling: wash and rinse, swirl in 1 teaspoon of unscented bleach per quart of water so it touches every surface, wait at least 30 seconds, then pour it out and let the container air-dryThis solution is for cleaning containers only. Never drink it.
  • Label each container with the date you filled it
  • Replace water you bottled yourself every 6 months

If you run short

  • Don't ration drinking water unless officials tell you to. Drink what you need today and look for more for tomorrow.
  • Cut how much your body needs by resting and staying cool.
  • Skip alcohol and caffeinated drinks: they make dehydration worse.
  • Your water heater tank holds usable water, and so does the toilet tank (not the bowl) if the water is clear and hasn't been treated with toilet cleaners. Our water guide shows how to get it out and how to treat any water you're unsure of.

The food side

FEMA suggests at least several days of non-perishable food. Store what your household already eats: canned meat, fish, beans, soup, fruit and vegetables, peanut butter, crackers, cereal, nuts and shelf-stable milk, plus a manual can opener. Put new cans at the back and use the oldest first. Our prepping for beginners guide has a full list.

Quick answers

How much water should I store for an emergency?

At least 1 gallon per person per day for at least 3 days, the CDC's minimum, for drinking, cooking and brushing teeth. A 2-week supply is better if you have the space. Store more for hot weather, which can double the water people need, and for children, pregnant women, nursing mothers, people who are sick, and pets.

How much water does a family of 4 need for 2 weeks?

56 gallons: 4 people × 14 days × 1 gallon. That's 56 one-gallon jugs, or about 18 cases of 24 half-liter bottles. In very hot weather, plan for up to twice as much.

How long does stored water last?

Store-bought bottled water lasts until its expiration date if it stays sealed in a cool, dark place. Water you bottle yourself in clean, food-grade containers should be replaced every 6 months.

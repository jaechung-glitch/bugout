How to prepare for a winter storm

A winter storm can cut off heat and power for days. Here's what to do before it hits, how to keep pipes from freezing, how to stay warm and safe if the power goes out, and what to keep in the car.

By the BugOut team. Updated November 5, 2026. Checked against Ready.gov, CDC, NHTSA, National Fire Protection Association and American Red Cross guidance.

The short answer

Before a winter storm, gather what you'd need to stay home for several days without power: water, food that needs no cooking, flashlights and batteries, a radio, medicines and warm blankets. Test your smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, protect your pipes from freezing, and fill the car's gas tank. During the storm, stay off the roads, never run a generator or grill indoors, and never heat your home with a gas oven or stovetop.

Watch, warning or advisory?

Know the three main winter weather alerts. Sign up for your community's warning system, and keep a NOAA Weather Radio or battery radio in case phones and power go down.

AlertWhat it means
Winter Storm WatchA blizzard, heavy snow, heavy freezing rain or heavy sleet is possible. Usually issued 12 to 48 hours ahead.
Winter Storm WarningHazardous winter weather is about to start or has started. Usually issued 12 to 24 hours ahead. Find shelter right away.
Winter Weather AdvisorySnow, freezing rain, freezing drizzle or sleet that will cause significant inconveniences, and could become life-threatening if you're not careful.

Before the storm: your home checklist

Plan to be on your own for several days. A winter storm can cut off heat, power and phone service, and older adults, children, people who are sick and pets are at greater risk.

  • Water: 1 gallon per person per day for at least 3 daysThe CDC's minimum, for drinking, cooking and brushing teeth. Our water calculator works out your total.
  • Food that needs no cooking or refrigeration, and a manual can opener
  • Flashlights or headlamps and extra batteriesUse flashlights, not candles, in the dark.
  • A battery-powered or hand-crank radio, and a NOAA Weather Radio with tone alert
  • Charged power banks and a car phone charger
  • Enough prescription medicine to last if you can't get out, plus a first-aid kit
  • A warm blanket or sleeping bag for each person, plus hats, gloves and warm layers
  • Food and extra water for pets
  • Smoke alarms and carbon monoxide alarms with battery backup, testedReady.gov lists carbon monoxide poisoning among the main dangers of winter storms.
  • Insulation, caulking and weather stripping to keep the cold out
  • Know where your main water shut-off valve isIf a pipe bursts, turning off the water fast limits the damage.

Can't afford heating bills or repairs to keep the cold out? The Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP) can help with heating costs and weatherization.

How to keep pipes from freezing

Water expands as it freezes, and the pressure can split metal or plastic pipes. Pipes freeze most often where they're exposed to cold: outdoor hose bibs, pool and sprinkler lines, pipes in unheated basements, crawl spaces, attics, garages and kitchen cabinets, and pipes along outside walls with little insulation.

Before cold weather

  • Disconnect, drain and store garden hoses
  • Close the inside valves that feed outdoor faucets, then open the outdoor faucets so they can drainLeave them open, so any water left in the pipe can expand without breaking it.
  • Drain pool and sprinkler supply lines the way the manufacturer or installer says, without adding antifreeze unless they tell you toAntifreeze is dangerous to people, pets and wildlife.
  • Insulate attics, basements and crawl spaces
  • Wrap exposed pipes with pipe sleeves or UL-listed heat tape or heat cableEven ¼ inch of newspaper gives some protection where freezes are rare and short.

On very cold nights

  • Let cold water drip from faucets served by exposed pipesEven a trickle of moving water helps stop a pipe freezing.
  • Open the cabinet doors under kitchen and bathroom sinks, after moving cleaners and chemicals out of children's reachWarm air reaches the pipes.
  • Keep the garage door closed if water lines run through the garage
  • Keep the thermostat at the same temperature day and night
  • Going away? Leave the heat on, set no lower than 55°F

If a pipe freezes

Only a trickle from the faucet usually means a frozen pipe, often along an outside wall or where the water line comes in through the foundation.

  1. Keep the faucet open. Running water helps melt the ice as it thaws.
  2. Warm that section of pipe with an electric heating pad wrapped around it, a hair dryer, a space heater kept away from anything that can burn, or towels soaked in hot water.
  3. Keep warming it until full water pressure comes back, then check your other faucets: if one pipe froze, others may have too.
  4. Call a licensed plumber if you can't find or reach the frozen section, or can't thaw it.

Never thaw a pipe with an open flame. Don't use a blowtorch, a kerosene or propane heater, a charcoal stove or any other open-flame device.

If the power goes out in the cold

Carbon monoxide is one of a winter storm's biggest dangers. Use generators and grills only outdoors, and never heat your home with a gas stovetop or oven. If the carbon monoxide alarm sounds, or anyone has a headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea or confusion, get everyone into fresh air right away, then call 911. People who are asleep can die before they notice anything.

  • Run a generator outdoors only, at least 20 feet from windows, doors and attached garages, and let it cool before refueling.
  • Keep space heaters at least 3 feet from anything that can burn, and turn them off when you leave the room or go to bed.
  • Use the blankets or sleeping bags from your kit, and dress in warm layers.
  • If you can't keep warm at home, go somewhere with heat, such as a warming center.

Our power outage checklist covers the rest, including how to keep one room warm and how long food in the fridge and freezer stays safe.

Frostbite and hypothermia: the signs

Limit time outside, and wear layers when you go out. Watch everyone for these signs, especially older adults and children.

ConditionSignsWhat to do
HypothermiaShivering, exhaustion, confusion, fumbling hands, memory loss, slurred speech or drowsiness. A body temperature below 95°F is an emergency: call 911.Get to a warm room. Warm the center of the body first: chest, neck, head and groin. Keep the person dry and wrapped in warm blankets, including the head and neck.
FrostbiteNumbness, and white or grayish-yellow skin that feels firm or waxy, usually on the face, fingers or toes.Get to a warm room. Soak the area in warm water, or warm it with body heat. Don't massage it or use a heating pad.

Shoveling snow is hard work, and overexertion raises the risk of a heart attack. Take it slowly and rest often.

Driving and your car

  • Stay off the roads if you can. Check the forecast and road conditions before any trip.
  • Keep the gas tank full, or an electric car charged. A full tank also keeps the fuel line from freezing.
  • Pack a winter car kit: a shovel, ice scraper, sand or kitty litter, jumper cables, a flashlight, blankets and warm clothes, water, snacks and a phone charger. Our car emergency kit list has the full checklist.
  • If you're stranded, stay with the car. Tie something bright to the antenna or a window, and keep the dome light on. Clear snow from the exhaust pipe, and run the engine only now and then, just long enough to stay warm. Don't run it for long with the windows up or in an enclosed space.

Quick answers

How do you prepare for a winter storm?

Get supplies to stay home for several days without power: water (1 gallon per person per day for at least 3 days), food that needs no cooking, flashlights and extra batteries, a battery or hand-crank radio, medicines and warm blankets. Test your smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, protect pipes from freezing, fill your car's gas tank, and plan to stay off the roads.

How do I keep my pipes from freezing?

On very cold nights, let cold water drip from faucets served by exposed pipes, open the cabinet doors under sinks, keep the garage door closed, and keep the thermostat at the same temperature day and night, the American Red Cross advises. Before winter, insulate exposed pipes and drain outdoor hoses. If you go away, leave the heat on at 55°F or higher.

What is the difference between a winter storm watch and a warning?

A winter storm watch means a blizzard, heavy snow, heavy freezing rain or heavy sleet is possible, usually 12 to 48 hours ahead. A winter storm warning means hazardous winter weather is about to start or has started, and is usually issued 12 to 24 hours ahead. Under a warning, Ready.gov says to find shelter right away.

What temperature should I keep my house to stop pipes freezing?

If you'll be away in cold weather, the American Red Cross says to leave the heat on and set no lower than 55°F. While you're home, keep the thermostat at the same temperature day and night instead of turning it down overnight.

Can I use my gas oven to heat the house if the power goes out?

No. Ready.gov says never to heat your home with a gas stovetop or oven: it can cause carbon monoxide poisoning and fires. Use generators and grills outdoors only, and keep battery-powered carbon monoxide alarms near every sleeping area.

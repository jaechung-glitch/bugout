Gifts for preppers: 18 practical ideas that get used

The best prepper gifts fill a gap in someone's emergency kit. These ideas come from FEMA's Build a Kit list and federal safety guidance, with what to look for in each, and a few gifts to skip.

By the BugOut team. Updated November 5, 2026. Checked against Ready.gov, CDC, NHTSA and National Fire Protection Association guidance.

The short answer

Give something that fills a gap in their emergency kit. The most useful picks are a NOAA Weather Radio with tone alert, a headlamp, a power bank, a water filter with purification tablets, a well-stocked first-aid kit, a battery-powered carbon monoxide alarm, and a first-aid and CPR class. If you can, ask what's already in their kit, or check it against our bug out bag list.

Light, power and alerts

  1. A NOAA Weather Radio with tone alert. FEMA's kit list includes one, plus a battery or hand-crank radio. The tone alert sounds an alarm when a warning is issued, even in the middle of the night.
  2. A headlamp, with extra batteries. A flashlight is on FEMA's list; a headlamp keeps both hands free for cooking, first aid or a flat tire.
  3. A power bank and charging cables. FEMA's list says a cell phone with chargers and a backup battery. Pick a capacity that charges their phone several times, and a car charger for the glove box.
  4. A battery-powered lantern. Light for a whole room in a power outage, and far safer than candles.

Water

  1. A portable water filter, with purification tablets. The CDC says most portable filters remove parasites but not bacteria or viruses. For bacteria, look for an absolute pore size of 0.3 microns or smaller, and plan to filter, then use tablets or boil. Our water guide explains which filter ratings matter.
  2. Food-grade water containers. The CDC's minimum is 1 gallon per person per day for at least 3 days. Our water calculator shows how much a household needs.

First aid and safety

  1. A well-stocked first-aid kit. For a car kit, NHTSA suggests one that includes a tourniquet.
  2. A first-aid and CPR class. Skills last longer than gear. Look for one that covers bleeding control too.
  3. A battery-powered carbon monoxide alarm. Ready.gov recommends carbon monoxide detectors with battery backup, and lists carbon monoxide poisoning among the main dangers of winter storms.
  4. A fire extinguisher. On FEMA's list of extra supplies.
  5. A whistle and a dust mask. Both are on FEMA's basic list: one to signal for help, the other to help filter contaminated air.

Warmth and shelter

  1. A warm sleeping bag or wool blanket. FEMA suggests one for each person in the household.
  2. A winter car kit. A snow shovel, ice scraper, blankets and a bag of kitty litter for traction. See our car emergency kit list.
  3. Matches in a waterproof container. A small gift from FEMA's list that's easy to forget.

Food, tools and paperwork

  1. A manual can opener and a mess kit. Both are on FEMA's list. Canned food is no help without a way to open it.
  2. A multi-tool, or a wrench and pliers. FEMA's list includes a wrench or pliers to turn off utilities.
  3. A waterproof document pouch. For copies of IDs, insurance and medical information, which FEMA suggests keeping in a waterproof, portable container.
  4. A good backpack for a go bag. Comfortable straps and room for 72 hours of supplies. Pair it with a printed bug out bag checklist.

Gifts to skip

  • Fuel-burning heaters or stoves for indoor heat. A camp stove, grill, or outdoor propane or kerosene heater must never be used indoors: they can cause carbon monoxide poisoning. If you give a space heater, choose an electric one, and remind them to keep it 3 feet from anything that can burn.
  • Candles as emergency light. Flashlights and lanterns are safer, especially with kids and pets moving around in the dark.
  • Food they won't eat. Emergency food works best when it's food the household already likes, rotated through everyday meals.

Quick answers

What is a good gift for a prepper?

Something that fills a gap in their emergency kit: a NOAA Weather Radio with tone alert, a headlamp, a power bank, a portable water filter with purification tablets, a well-stocked first-aid kit, a battery-powered carbon monoxide alarm, a fire extinguisher, or a first-aid and CPR class. Ask what's already in their kit first.

What is a good gift for someone starting an emergency kit?

A sturdy bag or bin with the basics from FEMA's Build a Kit list: a flashlight and extra batteries, a battery or hand-crank radio, a first-aid kit, a whistle, a manual can opener, a dust mask, and a phone power bank. Add our printable bug out bag checklist so they can fill in the rest.

Are survival gifts good for people who aren't preppers?

Yes, if they're useful in everyday emergencies like power outages, winter storms and car breakdowns. A headlamp, a power bank, a car emergency kit or a battery-powered carbon monoxide alarm gets used by almost anyone.

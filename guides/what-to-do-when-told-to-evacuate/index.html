What to do when you're told to evacuate

Hurricanes, wildfires, floods and chemical spills can all end with an order to leave. People who leave early, with a bag already packed and a place to go, have the easiest time. This guide covers what to take, what to do on the way out, and how to get there safely.

By the BugOut team. Updated November 12, 2026. Checked against American Red Cross, CAL FIRE, National Weather Service and Military OneSource guidance.

The short answer
  1. Leave as soon as you're told to, or sooner if you feel unsafe. Don't wait to see if things improve.
  2. Grab your go bag, medicines, documents, phone chargers, cash and pets.
  3. If there's time, lock up and turn off utilities as officials instruct.
  4. Follow the official evacuation route. Never drive through floodwater or around barriers.
  5. Tell someone outside the area where you're going.

When to leave

Authorities order a mandatory evacuation when conditions are severely hazardous and advise one when the danger is lower. Either way, leave early: the Red Cross says to go early enough to avoid being trapped by severe weather, and CAL FIRE says it's safer to leave a wildfire before an order is issued. Gas stations may close, so keep your tank at least half full whenever an evacuation is possible.

No car? Plan now: arrange a ride with family or friends, and ask your local emergency management office whether it provides evacuation transport.

What to take

  • Your go bag: at least 3 days of water, food and suppliesSee our bug out bag list.
  • Medicines, glasses and medical devices with chargers
  • IDs, insurance papers and medical records, or copies
  • Cash and cards
  • Phones, chargers and power banks
  • A change of clothes, sturdy shoes and protective clothing
  • Maps saved on your phone and on paper
  • Pet food, water, leashes, carriers and records
  • Baby supplies, if you need them

If you have time before you leave

  • Tell someone outside the area that you're leaving and where you're going
  • Lock doors and windows (for a wildfire, close them but leave them unlocked)
  • Turn off utilities and unplug appliances if officials tell you to
  • Bring in outdoor furniture and anything the wind could throw
  • Leave a note saying when you left and where you went

On the road

  • Follow the recommended evacuation route; shortcuts may be blocked or unsafe
  • Watch for downed trees, washed-out roads and power lines
  • Never drive into floodwater or around barricades
  • Keep the radio on for updates

Where to go

  • Family or friends outside the danger area are the most comfortable option. Agree on this before an emergency.
  • Hotels fill fast, so book early and note pet-friendly ones along your route.
  • Public shelters are listed on the free Red Cross Emergency app and announced on local news. Many accept only service animals.

Going home

  • Return only when officials say it's safe.
  • Watch for downed power lines, broken gas lines, floodwater and damaged buildings.
  • If you smell gas, leave and call 911 or your gas company from outside.
  • Restock your go bag once you're settled, so it's ready next time.

Quick answers

What should you do when told to evacuate?

Leave right away, by the route officials give. Take your go bag, medicines, documents, phone chargers and pets. If there's time, lock up, turn off utilities if told to, and tell someone outside the area where you're going. Don't drive through floodwater or around barriers.

What is the difference between a mandatory and a voluntary evacuation?

Authorities order a mandatory evacuation when conditions are severely hazardous, and advise (voluntary) evacuation when the danger is lower. Either way, leaving early is safer: roads get crowded and conditions can change fast.

What should you take when you evacuate?

Your go bag with at least 3 days of water, food and supplies; medicines and glasses; IDs, insurance papers and cash; phone chargers; a change of clothes and sturdy shoes; and food, leashes and carriers for pets.

Can you bring pets to an evacuation shelter?

Many public shelters accept only service animals, the Red Cross notes. Plan ahead with a list of pet-friendly hotels, friends and animal shelters along your route, and never leave pets behind.

Should you turn off utilities when you evacuate?

Follow local instructions. The National Weather Service says to disconnect utilities and appliances if you have time before a flood, and CAL FIRE says to turn off gas at the meter before leaving for a wildfire. If you turn the gas off, a professional must turn it back on.
